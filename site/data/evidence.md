# AI Evidence Log: the SIP-continuity discovery engine (Groww)

**What this is.** The evidence behind the AI discovery engine built for the brief *"increase the share of first-time mutual-fund investors who still have an active SIP 12 months after starting"*. It covers what the engine read, how often it agreed with a hand-labelled golden set and where it failed, and where AI output was rejected or corrected. Every number below is generated by script from the project's result files (`analysis/build_evidence_log_v1.py` for sections 1–4 and 6, `analysis/mvp_golden_section_v1.py` for section 5, `analysis/evidence_additions_v1.py` for sections 7–9); none is typed by hand.

**Who labelled.** All human labels are by the researcher, by hand, before seeing any model output. AI was used only to translate a few non-English comments and to tidy the wording of free-text notes, never to choose a label.

## At a glance

- **Read:** 1,631,157 clean public records from Reddit, YouTube, app-store reviews, TradingQ&A, X and social sites; 232,395 checked by the AI relevance gate; 90,755 analysed in depth; 32,868 of them are early-journey stories, the core of the problem.
- **Golden set:** 20 hand-labelled items run once, after all tuning. Relevance agreed on 17/20. The engine never invented an action (0 filled where the researcher saw none) but found only 2 of 8 real ones, so action counts are lower bounds. Its weakest field is telling an own story from a question (7/20); those errors push action counts down, never up.
- **Other checks:** a bigger model (Sonnet) agreed on actions 77% of the time; the researcher confirmed 16/20, 19/19 and 15/20 of the stories behind the three headline findings.
- **Rejected or corrected:** a prompt that made the engine invent actions (replaced by a code rule), an AI theme list that failed human agreement twice (demoted), and stories wrongly counted as payment problems (area narrowed), plus the smaller fixes in section 4.
- **Added after the submission (14 Nov 2026):** which of the brief's nine questions the engine answers and where it is thin (section 7); how the five opportunity areas are defined, and a re-run without the AI themes, in which no verdict changes (section 8); how the engine's answers differ from general AI (section 9).

## Terms used on this page

- **Record:** one public post, comment or review, as collected.
- **Relevance gate (gate):** the first AI pass. It reads a record and decides whether it is about SIPs or mutual funds. *Gate-positive* means it said yes.
- **Extraction:** the second AI pass. It fills the codebook fields (what the person did, what triggered it, what confused them) and must quote the record word for word for each one.
- **Episode or story:** one gate-positive record that extraction read in depth.
- **Early-journey:** a story by a first-time investor, or by someone acting within 12 months of starting. This is the core of the brief.
- **Stop, considering, kept going:** what the writer says they did, counted only when the second read confirms it (the "strict" labels).
- **Second read (J1):** a second AI pass that reads each action story with its post date and confirms an action only when the writer says they took it.
- **Golden set, dev and held-out:** items the researcher labelled by hand to grade the engine. *Dev* items were used while choosing the model and prompt; *held-out* items were kept back and run once, at the end.
- **Prompt versions** (gate_v1_1, extract_v1_2a, v1.3a): numbered versions of the instructions given to the AI.
- **Rules H1–H11:** checks written in code that run after the AI. H11 keeps an action only when the record is the writer's own story (section 4.1).
- **κ (kappa):** how often two labellers agree, after removing the agreement expected by chance. 1 is perfect and 0 is chance; the bar here was 0.6.
- **95% CI:** the range the true share most likely lies in, given the sample size.
- **Separates (✓), ~ and ✗:** ✓ means stop stories show something more often than continue stories from the same source, and the gap is above zero with 95% confidence; ~ means the gap could be zero; ✗ means continuers show it more.
- **Themes:** the AI's grouping of the needs people state (for example "Money stuck"). Demoted after they failed human agreement (sections 4.2 and 8).
- **Weight 2.0 and targeted top-up:** sampled general questions count twice, so the sample stands for all of them; the top-up is an extra search for rare stories and is kept out of every share.
- **Snapshot v1.10 and seed 20261029:** the frozen copy of the data, and the fixed random number that lets anyone draw the same items again.

**Where it failed**
1. **Own story vs question (type 7/20).** The most common miss: 6 posts that tell the writer's own story and also ask something were read as plain questions (G01, G02, G06, G11, G18, G27).
2. **Actions are under-counted, never invented.** Of the 8 items where the researcher saw a real action, the engine found 2; it filled an action where the researcher saw none 0 times. Of the 5 missed actions, 3 (G06, G18, G27) were removed by rule H11 (actions are kept only from own-experience items) because of miss 1. 1 action got a different code (G10: engine redeemed_withdrew, researcher switched_platform). **So every action count in the findings is a lower bound.**
3. **App features and in-app confusion.** The engine left the feature unknown where the researcher saw one in 4 items (3 of them customer support that didn't resolve). In-app confusion disagreed on 6 items: the engine saw confusion where the researcher saw none in 2 (G06, G34), missed it in 2 (G16, G40) and chose a different code in 2 (G24, G38).
4. **Relevance (17/20).** 3 misses: G01 (researcher a, engine r), G25 (researcher r, engine a), G34 (researcher a, engine r).

**What we did about it.** Type is never reported as a finding, and its errors only remove actions (rule H11), never add them. Action counts are reported as lower bounds, and a second, date-aware read (section 3) re-checks every action story. Feature and confusion fields are used only for direction, with the human check in section 3.

## 4. Where we rejected or corrected AI output

### 4.1 A better-sounding prompt made the engine invent actions, so we replaced it with a rule
- **What happened.** The extraction model filled "what the person did" from plans and questions ("should I stop?"). On the 20 dev items, prompt v1.2a got 15/20 actions right but filled an action where the researcher saw none 5 times.
- **The fix we tried and rejected.** Prompt v1.3a added an instruction against this. It made things worse: 11/20 right, 7 invented, and only 5/7 real actions found. We rejected it and stopped tuning prompts on 20 items.
- **What replaced it.** Rule H11, in code: keep an action only if the item is an own-experience story. On the dev items: 17/20 right, 2 invented. On the untouched held-out items: 0 invented, at the cost of finding 2 of 8 real actions (section 2). We chose a conservative engine over an inventive one.

### 4.2 The AI's theme list did not survive human agreement, so it was demoted
- **What happened.** The model sorted every stated unmet need into themes. Before trusting them, the researcher and Claude labelled the same items blind. Round 1 (60 items): researcher–Claude κ 0.29, researcher–model κ 0.39. The codebook was rewritten with decision rules; round 2 (40 fresh items): κ 0.36 and 0.32.
- **Why it failed.** Claude and the model agreed with each other (κ 0.82) but not with the human: the categories reflected how a model reads text, not how an investor means it (for example, "which funds" hid portfolio reviews, and there was no theme for return expectations).
- **What we did.** Themes are shown only as colour on the dashboard, and no finding is reported from them alone. *Corrected on 14 Nov 2026:* this line used to say that every headline finding rests on the structured fields validated in sections 2 and 3. In fact the rules that define the five opportunity areas also counted a story when its theme named the matching need. Section 8 re-runs every area without the themes: no verdict changes.

### 4.3 A human check caught the engine counting the wrong stories as payment problems
- **What happened.** In the check of area A (payment & AutoPay problems), 2 of the researcher's "no" notes were KYC blocks, and 1 story the engine counted was about an insurance premium, not a mutual fund. Several "yes" stories had no timing, so "first months" was not proven.
- **What we did.** Area A was narrowed and renamed "payment & AutoPay problems (early-journey stories)"; a strict reading of its score is one lower. KYC (raised 3 times across the notes) has no code in the engine, so it became an interview question.

### Other corrections (all logged)

|---|---|---|

| The dashboard's first build | Its "real examples" on the try-it page were hand-written; numbers were typed into the build script | Rejected before going public; rebuilt from the engine's own outputs |

## 5. The MVP's own golden set (Sensecheck)

**What is tested.** Sensecheck is the AI feature in the SIP Passbook MVP: it answers a first-year investor's question about one of their SIP months in plain words, using only that passbook's facts and Groww's help rules, and it must decline anything that asks for investment advice. Every answer is checked in code before it is shown; if a check fails, the investor sees a fixed answer built from the passbook instead.

**The golden set.** 20 hand-labelled questions (version 1.0). Claude drafted the labels; the researcher checked every item and approved the set on 2026-11-07 before the run. Each item fixes the month it is about, the expected intent, phrases an acceptable answer must contain, the help sources it may cite, and *critical* patterns (for example, saying units were allotted when they were not). 4 items ask for advice and must be declined.

**Scoring.** Not a fallback (gate_status "checked"; for advice items a decline, by the model or the pre-filter), intent in expected_intent, every must_include group has at least one phrase in the answer (case-insensitive), sources_any (when given) shares at least one id with the answer's sources, no must_not pattern, no critical failure.

**Latest live run** (eval-20261109021635, claude-haiku-5-5, 2026-11-09): **18/20 acceptable (90%, threshold 90%)**, **0 critical failures** (allowed: 0), 1 of 20 answers replaced by the fixed fallback, advice declined 4/4.

**The gate.** The MVP's build refuses to deploy unless this run's prompt and golden-set hashes match the files being deployed and every threshold is met (`scripts/check-eval.mjs` in the MVP repo). Changing the prompt or a label means a new live run.

## 6. What this evidence cannot show
- People who quietly let a SIP lapse rarely post, so the size of quiet drift is unknown. The interviews test it.
- Public posts over-represent loud failures; shares compare groups within one source, not the population.
- Income-stability cells stay small even after the targeted top-up.

## 8. How the five opportunity areas are defined, and a re-run without the AI themes

**What an area is.** The five areas group the problems the nine questions surfaced among stop stories: the routes to a stop (Q3), and what confused stoppers in the app and what they tried there (Q5). Each area is a rule over the codebook fields that extraction fills, each backed by a word-for-word quote (`areas()` in `analysis/defs.py`).

**How each area was tested.** Within each source, do stop stories show the area more often than continue stories, with 95% confidence? Sources are kept apart because app reviews hold many stop stories and few continue stories. For A, B and C the researcher also read 20 random stop stories from the area as published (section 3).

| Area | A story counts when… | Theme labels that also counted |
|---|---|---|
| A. Payment & AutoPay problems | the writer describes a failed payment or a mandate problem, a first-debit problem or gap, or is confused about AutoPay or the SIP date and first debit | "Payments and AutoPay" |
| B. Where is my money, and support that resolves | the writer is confused about where their money went or about order status and units, or contacted customer support and it did not resolve or they could not reach it | "Money stuck", "Support that resolves", "Orders, NAV and units" |
| C. Returns vs expectations | the writer describes negative, flat or confusing returns, was triggered by disappointing returns or market-fall news, saw a loss, or is confused about how returns are calculated or why they are negative | "Stay, pause or stop?" |
| D. Cash-flow shocks | the writer was triggered by a cash crunch, an expense or an income change, or describes a life event or cash need | "Fit the SIP to my cash flow" |

## 9. How the engine's answers differ from general AI

*Copied from the Part 1 findings, section 7 (`docs/analysis/findings_part1.md`), where every number is read from the engine's result files.*

Six write-ups of Parts 1–2 made with general AI tools (web search plus synthesis) were compared with the engine, claim by claim (`docs/research/ai_baselines_comparison_v1.md`). Five of the six tell one story: disappointing returns erode confidence, people stop in months 3–9, influencer-led youngsters are most at risk, and goals and reassurance fix it. The engine agrees with parts of it and corrects the rest.

| What general AI says | What the engine shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Disappointing returns are why people stop | Losses drive *considering* (55% of that group describe negative returns), but stoppers and continuers mention them at the same rate (21% vs 21%); "market fall" is the trigger for 5% of stoppers and 10% of continuers | Explains who wobbles, not who leaves |
| Months 3–9 are the critical window | 49% of first-time stoppers who give a timing stopped in months 0–3; considering peaks at months 7–12 (30%) | Stops come earlier |
| Failed debits matter but can't be sized | Payment & AutoPay problems separate stoppers from continuers on all three sources (section 8) | Agree; the engine adds the size signal |
| Influencer-led starters stop most | Stop share: started via creator/YouTube 17% [8%–32%], n=39; own research 28% [24%–33%], n=492 | Not supported (small n) |
| Salary-date mismatch, over-commitment and bounce fees cause cancellations | In stoppers' extracted evidence: salary timing 0 of 684, amount too high 5 of 684, bounce/ECS charges 6 of 684 | Barely visible in public text; tested in interviews |
| People cancel AutoPay in GPay/PhonePe | 2 of 684 stoppers' evidence mentions it | Not seen; one interview probe |
| Goals keep people going | Continuers' reasons: long-term view 71%, goal reminder 2% | A long-term frame protects; explicit goals are rarely the reason |

### Four further checks
- **Stoppers go quieter.** Outside the app, stoppers more often did nothing (35% vs 30% of continuers) and less often asked a community (Reddit/forums 23% vs 35%); when they did reach out, it was more often an advisor (9%) or the bank (8%). n = 114 stop, 136 continue: indicative.
- **XIRR readers stay.** Continuers most often check XIRR (34%); those considering stopping watch % returns (40%) and ₹ gain/loss (33%), and only 15% XIRR. n = 145 continue, 60 considering.
- **The headline is not a market-crash artefact.** Only 36 of 684 early-journey stop stories were posted in March–May 2026, the correction window. In that window returns disappointment (31%) and cash flow (19%) are larger stop routes than in other months (20%, 8%), and payment lapse smaller (22% vs 36%). Market state matters, but it does not drive the corpus-wide picture.
- **Money source.** Stop share among early-journey stories that state where the money came from: family money 36% [23%–51%], n=46; salary 21% [17%–27%], n=269; pocket money 12% [6%–23%], n=56. Small cells: a direction for the interviews, not a finding.
